Arco for macOS

Frequently asked questions.

Direct, current answers about what Arco does, where meeting data goes, how Codex and Claude are used, and what “local-first” means in practice.

What is Arco?

Arco is an open-source, local-first AI meeting workspace for macOS. It keeps a live, speaker-labeled transcript beside Codex or Claude so you can ask questions, challenge an assumption, or recover a decision while the meeting is still happening.

How is Arco different from a conventional AI meeting notetaker?

Conventional meeting notetakers primarily produce transcripts and summaries for review after a call. Arco can do both, but its defining workflow is live reasoning: the transcript remains visible as evidence while the Agent helps during the conversation. Arco also uses the Codex or Claude CLI already on your Mac instead of requiring a separate hosted Arco chatbot.

Does Arco send a bot into Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams?

No. Arco runs as a native macOS application and captures system audio plus the selected room microphone. It does not join the call as a participant, so it works independently of the meeting app.

Can Arco transcribe meetings entirely on-device?

Yes. Arco supports on-device ASR with Nemotron or Whisper and on-device speaker separation with Sortformer, Pyannote + WeSpeaker, or LS-EEND. The selected models must be downloaded before capture. You can also choose Deepgram, Doubao Speech, or ElevenLabs when a cloud service better fits the meeting.

Does on-device transcription mean all of Arco is offline?

No. On-device recognition and speaker separation can keep speech processing on the Mac after the required models are installed. Agent questions still follow the network and data policy of the Codex or Claude service you selected. A cloud transcription provider also requires a network connection.

How does Arco use Codex or Claude?

Arco sends each question through the Codex CLI or Claude Code already installed and authenticated on the Mac. Every question includes the current meeting transcript. A project workspace is included only when you attach it visibly with the native macOS folder picker. Follow-ups resume the exact provider session bound to that meeting and context.

Do I need an OpenAI or Anthropic API key?

Not for Agent features. When the local Codex or Claude CLI is signed in, Arco uses that CLI and the subscription allowance associated with it. Cloud transcription providers use their own credentials and may have separate usage costs.

Where does Arco store transcripts and notes?

By default, Arco stores meeting data under ~/Library/Application Support/Arco/. Transcripts and notes are readable Markdown files, with local sidecars for meeting state and Agent bindings. You can choose other local folders for transcripts and notes.

Does Arco save raw meeting audio?

No. Arco does not save raw PCM meeting audio. During a meeting, audio is streamed only to the transcription path you selected: an on-device worker or the configured cloud provider.

How does speaker labeling work?

Arco keeps system audio and the room microphone as separate source lanes. Speaker separation runs inside each lane, producing labels such as Remote 1 and In room 1. Those labels describe source and diarization slots; Arco does not assume the microphone speaker is “You” or claim a durable human identity.

Is Arco free and open source?

Yes. Arco is free to download and its source code is published under the MIT License. Optional transcription providers may charge for their own service usage. Codex or Claude Agent usage follows the account already signed into that CLI.

Which Macs can run Arco?

Arco requires macOS 14 or later. The current prebuilt download targets Apple Silicon. On-device speech and speaker models are designed for Apple Silicon; developers can inspect the build requirements in the repository.

Is the current download notarized by Apple?

Not yet. The current preview uses Arco's stable local development signature. On first launch, Control-click the app, choose Open, and confirm once. A future generally available release is expected to add Developer ID signing and notarization.

Can I inspect the privacy and architecture claims?

Yes. Arco is open source. Start with the privacy boundary, the architecture overview, and the complete repository.
